\begin{abstract}
The electronic charge density $\rho(\mathbf{r})$ computed by density-functional
theory (DFT) encodes the bonding that materials-property models try to learn,
yet it is rarely used directly as a model input: machine-learning (ML)
featurizers are mostly built on composition and structure, and density-based
analyses such as the quantum theory of atoms in molecules are applied one
structure at a time. We present pydemi, an open-source Python framework that
turns volumetric charge densities into physically interpretable descriptors in
a single batch pipeline. pydemi is organized around one data model and a
periodic geometry pass: derived fields --- the magnetization, an electron
localization function reconstructed from the density, the electrostatic
potential and the deformation density --- are computed once, cached, and
reduced by shared moment, shell-fraction, anisotropy, site-aggregation and
topology operators. The current release computes 220 scalar descriptors in
five domains --- bonding, structural, magnetic, site heterogeneity and,
adopted from matminer, composition --- each registered with its formula,
units, range and documented values for degenerate cases, and each tested for
invariance under supercell expansion, translation and rotation. pydemi reads
densities from VASP and, through cube and XSF files, from other plane-wave
codes, and is designed with a second input path for charge densities
predicted by ML directly from crystal structures.
% TODO: revise the previous sentence once the CIF -> CHGCAR model is integrated.
On a 6,059-structure VASP dataset we quantify how numerical choices affect
the descriptors. Projector-augmented-wave pseudo-densities require dedicated
definitions: 61\% of the densities are negative somewhere, pseudized atoms can
lack a density maximum at the nucleus, 87\% of the deformation density lies
inside the augmentation spheres, and valence counts inferred from electron
configurations disagree with the PAW datasets for 43 of 87 elements. Two
descriptor families are not converged on typical grids: the voxel-averaged
bond ellipticity changes by 20--70\% between derivative schemes, and the
critical-point census closes in only 9\% of the structures, the mismatch being
exactly the number of stencil-dependent extrema. pydemi flags both and
provides a converged, bounded ellipticity. The implementation is validated by
876 automated tests against analytic densities, exact identities and
reference data, and processes the full dataset without failure in 37 CPU
hours.
\end{abstract}

\section{Numerical considerations}
\label{sec:numerics}

Every descriptor in pydemi is a reduction over a sampled density, so its value
depends on how derivatives are discretized, how voxels are assigned to atoms,
and how the underlying DFT code represents the density near the nuclei. This
section quantifies these effects on real VASP charge densities and describes
the definitions pydemi adopts as a result. Statistics are computed on the
6,059-structure dataset described in Section~\ref{sec:application}, or on
random samples of it with the sample size stated (grid spacings
0.055--0.068\,\AA, 5th--95th percentile). The analysis scripts are
distributed with the paper, so that every number below can be regenerated.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Derivatives on a periodic grid}
\label{sec:numerics:derivatives}

Gradients, Hessians and Laplacians are evaluated on the periodic grid with the
exact metric tensor: with reciprocal vectors $\mathbf{b}_a$ and fractional
coordinates $u_a$, $\nabla f=\sum_a\mathbf{b}_a\,\partial f/\partial u_a$ and
$\nabla^2 f=\sum_{ab}G_{ab}\,\partial^2 f/\partial u_a\partial u_b$ with
$G=BB^{\mathsf T}$. Non-orthogonal cells are thus treated without dropping the
cross terms. Two backends are provided: exact derivatives of the trigonometric
interpolant (FFT, the default), and central differences of order 2, 4, 6 or 8.
Table~\ref{tab:derivatives} compares them on 200 random structures, taking the
FFT result as the reference.

For most descriptors the finite-difference results converge to the FFT result
as the order increases: fourth-order differences agree with it to about
$10^{-4}$ (median), second-order ones to 0.05--0.8\%, with 95th percentiles
of 0.3--2\% (10\% for the bond-shell ELF fraction). The FFT backend is therefore the converged limit on
these grids, and second-order differences carry errors at the percent level
for individual structures. The exceptions are the ELF gradient anisotropy and
the bond ellipticity, which change by 20--64\% whatever the order and do not
converge (Section~\ref{sec:numerics:convergence}). Dropping the off-diagonal
metric terms of the Laplacian, a shortcut that is exact only for orthogonal
cells, changes the Laplacian-based descriptors of non-orthogonal cells by a
median 3--12\%, and by more than 70\% in the worst cases. pydemi keeps it only as an option to quantify
this effect. As in the derivative comparison, the voxel-count fraction
$f_{\nabla^2\rho<0}=N^{-1}\sum_k\mathbf{1}(\nabla^2\rho_k<0)$ is more sensitive
than its charge-weighted counterpart
$f^{\rho}_{\nabla^2\rho<0}=\sum_{\nabla^2\rho_k<0}\rho_k/\sum_k\rho_k$ (11\%
against 4\%). In flat interstitial regions many voxels have
$\nabla^2\rho\approx0$, and their sign flips with small changes of the
operator; weighting by the density suppresses them. pydemi reports both.

The round-off error of the FFT Laplacian is absolute, about $10^{-16}$ of the
largest value in the cell, so in near-empty regions its sign carries no
information. This does not affect pydemi's descriptors, which are
charge-weighted or restricted to voxels above a density floor. For an
isolated Gaussian density in a 7\,\AA{} box, the FFT and fourth-order results of
every bonding descriptor agree to within 0.2\%, whereas second-order
differences shift the bond-shell ELF fraction by 33\%. The FFT backend does
ring near nuclear cusps, however, so for all-electron densities
(\texttt{AECCAR0} + \texttt{AECCAR2}) finite differences should be used.

One descriptor is affected not by discretization but by periodicity itself.
The Laplacian-concentration ratio $\sum_{\nabla^2\rho<0}|\nabla^2\rho_k| /
\sum_k|\nabla^2\rho_k|$ is identically $\tfrac{1}{2}$ for any periodic density.
By the divergence theorem the cell integral of $\nabla^2\rho$ vanishes, so its
negative and positive parts balance exactly. The same holds for the discrete
operators: the periodic stencil telescopes, and the FFT Laplacian has no
$\mathbf{G}=0$ component. Over the 200 structures the integrated discrete
Laplacian never exceeds $5\times10^{-14}$ of $\int|\nabla^2\rho|\,dV$, and over
all 6,059 the ratio equals $\tfrac{1}{2}$ to $9\times10^{-16}$. pydemi keeps this
entry, as the specification defines it, and adds a variant restricted to the
voxels outside the core shell, where the flux through the shell boundary
breaks the balance.

Finally, distances to the nearest nucleus are computed with a periodic image
search whose range is extended until it provably contains every voxel's
nearest atom. Wrapping fractional coordinate differences to the nearest
integer, a common shortcut, is the minimum image only for near-orthogonal
cells and overestimates distances by more than 0.1\,\AA{} in strongly sheared
ones. When two images are equidistant, as for voxels on the facets of
symmetric Voronoi cells, the tie is broken by a geometric rule (the largest
fractional displacement), and shell boundaries are tested with a
$10^{-8}$\,\AA{} tolerance. Without these, atoms on high-symmetry grid points
make the site assignment depend on round-off, and the descriptors change
under a supercell expansion or a translation of the origin. pydemi's test
harness checks every descriptor for invariance under both, and under
rotation, to $10^{-6}$.

\subsection{Partition schemes}
\label{sec:numerics:partitions}

Site-resolved descriptors require an assignment of voxels to atoms. pydemi
implements the nearest-atom (Voronoi) partition, its default, and a power
diagram weighted by covalent radii, both exact, and two smooth partitions:
Becke's fuzzy cells~\cite{becke1988} and Hirshfeld's stockholder
weights~\cite{hirshfeld1977}. The whole-cell descriptors do not depend on the
choice: they use the distance to the nearest nucleus for every voxel. Of the 88
descriptors of the bonding, structural, magnetic and heterogeneity domains, 63
are identical under all four partitions over 300 random structures, and the
other 25 are the site statistics of the heterogeneity domain and the site
magnetic moments.

Becke's weight of atom $i$ is a product over all other atoms,
$P_i=\prod_{j\neq i}s(\mu_{ij})$, of a switching function that approaches 1
only as $1-O\big((d/R)^8\big)$ for a distant atom at distance $R$. In a
molecule the product is finite; in a periodic solid the number of atoms at
distance $R$ grows as $R^2$, and the weights converge only algebraically with
the number of atoms retained (Table~\ref{tab:partitions}). pydemi therefore
truncates the product at the 60 nearest atom images, which leaves an error of
$1.5\times10^{-2}$ in the weights of FeNi$_3$. It assigns weight only to the
eight nearest images, which carry all of it, and renormalizes. Hirshfeld weights,
$w_i=\rho^{\mathrm{free}}_i/\sum_j\rho^{\mathrm{free}}_j$, inherit instead the
exponential decay of the free-atom densities. pydemi evaluates them from the
same periodic image sums as the promolecule, converged to
$10^{-6}$\,e\,\AA$^{-3}$, so that they form a partition of unity to
$1.3\times10^{-15}$.

The site statistics themselves change substantially with the partition
(Table~\ref{tab:partition-sensitivity}), because a voxel's distance is measured
to the atom that receives it. Becke cells, which like the nearest-atom
partition ignore atomic size, shift the site standard deviations of $m_1$,
$f_{\mathrm{bond}}$ and $\zeta$ by a median 8--16\% and preserve the ranking of
structures ($\rho_s \geq 0.97$). The power diagram and the Hirshfeld partition,
which give larger atoms larger regions, shift them by 24--51\% and reorder the
structures ($\rho_s$ down to 0.35 for the site spread of $m_1$). The site
magnetic moments are the exception: the spin density is concentrated near the
nuclei, and their spread changes by only 1--2\%. The site-resolved descriptors
are therefore properties of the density \emph{and} the partition, and pydemi
records the partition in the metadata of every row.

\subsection{Projector-augmented-wave pseudo-densities}
\label{sec:numerics:paw}

The \texttt{CHGCAR} file written by VASP holds the pseudo-valence density plus
the compensation charge of the projector-augmented-wave (PAW)
method~\cite{blochl1994,kresse1999}, not the all-electron density. Inside each
augmentation sphere this density is pseudized. Four aspects of the descriptor
definitions have to take that into account. pydemi collects the definitions
that exclude the spheres in an extension (\texttt{paw}) that is off by
default, so that the default descriptor set is the same for every input.

\paragraph{Negative densities.} In 3,680 of the 6,059 densities (61\%) the
pseudo-density is negative somewhere, and the median ratio of the density
minimum to the mean density is $-0.22$. The density floor
$\rho_{\min}/\langle\rho\rangle$, proposed as a metallicity criterion, is
therefore set by a PAW artefact rather than by the interstitial region.
The \texttt{paw} extension adds an interstitial floor, the minimum over voxels
farther than $\max(c_2, R_{\mathrm{PAW}})$ from their nucleus, where $c_2$ is
the interstitial shell radius and $R_{\mathrm{PAW}}$ the outermost PAW cutoff
radius (\texttt{RCORE}) read from the \texttt{POTCAR} or \texttt{OUTCAR}. This
floor is negative for only 3 structures, so in all others every negative value
lies inside the spheres. Its ratio to the mean density has a median of 0.13,
and it is undefined, and flagged, for 8 densely packed cells in which no voxel
lies outside the spheres.

\paragraph{Pseudized nuclei.} A pseudized atom need not have a density maximum
at its nucleus. In CaSi$_3$Pt the density has maxima at the Ca and Pt nuclei
but not at the Si nuclei, where it is negative ($-0.18$\,e\,\AA$^{-3}$ at
0.02\,\AA{} from Si); the Si valence density instead forms eight lobes 0.81 and
0.83\,\AA{} from the Si nuclei, holding 6.4 electrons. With the fixed
0.8\,\AA{} cutoff of the default definition these lobes are counted as eight
non-nuclear maxima holding 21\% of the valence charge. The \texttt{paw}
extension counts a maximum as non-nuclear only if it lies farther than
$\max(0.8\,\text{\AA}, R_{\mathrm{PAW}})$ from its nearest nucleus (for Si,
$R_{\mathrm{PAW}} = 1.01$\,\AA), since pseudization cannot move density maxima
outside the augmentation sphere; for CaSi$_3$Pt this leaves none. Across the
dataset, 2,264 structures have at least one non-nuclear maximum with the fixed
cutoff and 346 with the PAW-aware one.

\paragraph{Deformation density.} The deformation density
$\Delta\rho=\rho-\sum_i\rho^{\mathrm{free}}_i$ compares the crystal density
with a superposition of free atoms, taken from spherical LDA atoms tabulated
for $Z=1$--96 and summed over periodic images to $10^{-6}$\,e\,\AA$^{-3}$. For a
\texttt{CHGCAR} the free-atom reference must contain only the valence
electrons of the PAW dataset (ZVAL), but it retains the all-electron shape
inside the augmentation sphere. Table~\ref{tab:feni3} shows the consequence
around the Fe site of FeNi$_3$: beyond 0.8\,\AA{} the two densities agree to
within 4\%, whereas within 0.8\,\AA{} of the nucleus, well inside the
1.22\,\AA{} Fe augmentation sphere, the pseudization shifts about 0.48
electrons from $r<0.4$\,\AA{} to 0.4--0.8\,\AA. Over 121 random structures
with their own \texttt{OUTCAR}, a median 87\% (interquartile range 78--93\%) of
$\int|\Delta\rho|\,dV$ lies inside the augmentation spheres, which occupy 45\%
of the volume, while the charge inside the spheres differs from that of the
reference by only $-1.2\%$ (median; interquartile range $-1.9\%$ to $0.0\%$).
Deformation descriptors weighted by $|\Delta\rho|$ over the whole cell are
therefore dominated by the pseudization, although $\Delta\rho$ outside the
spheres reflects the bonding. With \texttt{AECCAR0} and \texttt{AECCAR2}, pydemi
uses the all-electron density against a reference of the frozen core
(\texttt{AECCAR0}) plus the tabulated free-atom valence densities, which is
consistent by construction; \texttt{AECCAR0} alone, the frozen core, cannot
serve as the promolecule. When only a \texttt{CHGCAR} is available, the
\texttt{paw} extension computes the deformation family over the voxels outside
the augmentation spheres, where the pseudo-density equals the all-electron
valence density. For the 6,059 structures these voxels make up 54\% of the
cell (median; 5th--95th percentile 24--76\%), and the deformation polarity
$\int|\Delta\rho|\,dV/Q$ over them is 2.3\% of the valence charge (median;
0.8--5.8\%).

\paragraph{Valence counts.} ZVAL fixes the electron count of the free-atom
reference, of the ionic charges in the electrostatic potential and of the
Hirshfeld $Z_i$ (below), so it must match the PAW datasets of the calculation.
pydemi reads it from the \texttt{POTCAR} or \texttt{OUTCAR}. Of the 6,059 runs,
1,158 have neither. For them a rule based on the electron configuration is a
poor substitute: counting the electrons outside the preceding noble-gas core
(and a filled $4f^{14}$ shell)
disagrees with the datasets used for 43 of the 87 elements, because it counts
the filled $d^{10}$ shell of the $p$-block (15 electrons for Sb instead of 5)
and misses the $5s^25p^6$ semicore of the lanthanides. The resulting reference
is then off by up to 80 electrons per cell. pydemi's fallback rule therefore
excludes the $p$-block $d^{10}$ shell and includes the semicore of the
lanthanides and actinides, which reproduces 78 of the 87 elements; the other
nine are semicore choices (K\_pv, Ca\_pv, Sr\_sv, Y\_sv, Zr\_sv, \ldots) that
no rule can infer. Runs without a \texttt{POTCAR} or \texttt{OUTCAR} can be
given a per-element table, which for this dataset is read from its 4,901
\texttt{OUTCAR} files; they use one PAW dataset per element throughout. The
origin of the counts is recorded for every structure. With these counts the
integrated deformation density $\int\Delta\rho\,dV$, which vanishes for exact
counts and exact sampling, has a median magnitude of 0.06 electrons per cell
(99th percentile 0.78).

\paragraph{Atomic charges.} The same mismatch affects atomic charges.
Hirshfeld weights built from the valence free-atom densities, with
$Z_i=\mathrm{ZVAL}_i$ so that the charges sum to zero, give a sign of charge
transfer that follows the Pauling electronegativity difference in only 18 of
31 binary compounds (58\%; 17 of the 26 with $\Delta\chi>0.4$). pydemi
therefore computes Hirshfeld charges from a \texttt{CHGCAR} but recommends the
all-electron density, or a reference built from isolated-atom calculations
with the same PAW datasets, in which the pseudization cancels.

\subsection{The critical-point census}
\label{sec:numerics:census}

pydemi counts critical points by direct comparison on the grid, without a
critical-point search. Maxima and minima are voxels higher or lower than all
26 neighbours. Saddles cannot be classified on that shell, which is not a
triangulated sphere: every adjacency on it miscounts the six saddles of
$\cos 2\pi x+\cos 2\pi y+\cos 2\pi z$. Their multiplicities are therefore taken
from the number of connected components of the lower and upper link on the
14-neighbour Freudenthal triangulation of the grid~\cite{freudenthal1942},
whose link is a triangulated sphere. Ties are broken by voxel index. A census
taken entirely on the Freudenthal link satisfies the Morse relation
$n_{\max}-n_{\mathrm{saddle},2}+n_{\mathrm{saddle},1}-n_{\min}=0$, the Euler
characteristic of the three-torus, for any sampled function~\cite{banchoff1970};
it did so for all 200 structures we tested. With the 26-neighbour extrema the
same alternating sum, which pydemi reports as \texttt{euler\_consistency} for
every structure, is therefore exactly
\begin{equation}
e = \big(n_{\max}^{(26)}-n_{\max}^{(14)}\big) - \big(n_{\min}^{(26)}-n_{\min}^{(14)}\big),
\end{equation}
the number of extrema whose status depends on the neighbourhood used; the
identity held for all 200 structures.

On the VASP pseudo-densities this number is rarely zero. It vanishes for 550
of the 6,059 structures (9.1\%), and its magnitude is a median 5\% of all
critical points (90th percentile 15\%); the median structure has 24 maxima and
10 minima on the Freudenthal stencil that are not 26-neighbour extrema. The
mismatches are not a matter of grid spacing, which has a median of
0.064\,\AA{} whether $e$ vanishes or not. They follow the PAW artefacts and the
low-amplitude ripple of nearly-free-electron densities: 63\% of the structures
with $e\neq0$ have a negative density somewhere, against 34\% of those with
$e=0$, and 39\% have a non-nuclear maximum, against 17\%. In such near-flat
regions a voxel can be higher than its 14 Freudenthal neighbours but not than
all 26, and whether it is a maximum is then a property of the stencil rather
than of the density. The ripple is most visible in the non-nuclear maxima:
Yb$_2$MgIn has 602 of them.

The consequence differs between the counts. The numbers of maxima and minima
are dominated by the nuclear maxima and the cage minima, and do not change for
the median structure when the grid is coarsened to 80\% of its points per
axis. The saddle counts change by a median 17--20\%
(Section~\ref{sec:numerics:convergence}). pydemi therefore reports all four
counts, as the per-volume densities $n/V$, together with
\texttt{euler\_consistency}. The two saddle counts are marked as numerically
fragile in the registry (Section~\ref{sec:numerics:convergence}). The charge
fraction in the basins of the non-nuclear maxima, $Q_{\mathrm{NNM}}$, and the
percolation thresholds are the topological descriptors that remain stable. The
percolation threshold $\rho_{\mathrm{perc},\alpha}$ is the highest density
level whose super-level set $\{\rho>c\}$ still spans direction $\alpha$; the
lowest spanning level is always the density minimum. A cluster spans only if
it winds around the cell, which is detected with a union--find over periodic
images. A cluster that merely touches both faces, such as a single blob across
the periodic boundary, does not span; testing for face contact instead would
make the threshold depend on the choice of the cell origin.

\subsection{Grid convergence and descriptor stability}
\label{sec:numerics:convergence}

Whether a grid resolves a descriptor can be tested directly: pydemi recomputes
the descriptors on a Fourier-coarsened copy of the grid and reports the
relative change of each. For 30 random structures and the 88 descriptors of
the bonding, structural, magnetic and heterogeneity domains, 44\% change by at
most 0.1\% (median over the structures) when the number of grid points along
each axis is reduced to 80\%, and 83\% by at most 1\%. The radial moments,
the shell fractions, the energy densities, the ELF averages, the
information-theoretic measures, the anisotropy tensor, the site potentials,
the bond-midpoint densities and the percolation thresholds belong to this
group. Per structure, a median 20\% of the descriptors (range 11--36\%) change
by more than 2\%.

The large changes fall into three classes. The first are quantities that are
small differences of large numbers or near zero by symmetry: the percolation
anisotropy (19\%), which vanishes for cubic cells, and the mean pair charge
transfer (43\%), whose typical magnitude is 0.009 electrons. Their absolute
changes are small, and they should be judged by absolute rather than relative
error. The second are the within-element site variances (63--75\%). When all
sites of an element are equivalent by symmetry, as for 3,721 of the 5,638
structures with a repeated element, the true variance is zero and the computed
value is numerical noise: the within-element variance of $m_1$ has a median of
$2\times10^{-9}$\,\AA$^2$ there, about 5,000 times below its median of
$1.2\times10^{-5}$\,\AA$^2$ for structures with inequivalent sites. These
descriptors carry information only for chemically or structurally disordered
cells. The third class is genuinely unconverged: the saddle counts
(Section~\ref{sec:numerics:census}) and the bond ellipticity.

The ellipticity statistics defined by the specification, the mean and
standard deviation of $\epsilon=\lambda_1/\lambda_2-1$ over the bond-shell
voxels with $\lambda_2<0$, are the least stable of all descriptors, with median
changes of 45\% and 87\% on the coarsened grid. The ratio diverges as
$\lambda_2\to0^-$, which happens at many bond-shell voxels away from the bond
paths, where the ratio is then set by the last digits of $\lambda_2$. Unlike
the QTAIM ellipticity, which is evaluated at the bond critical point, the
voxel average therefore depends on every numerical choice.
Table~\ref{tab:ellipticity} compares the specified statistics with three robust
alternatives over 48 random structures. The median of $\epsilon$ and the mean
of the bounded form $1-\lambda_2/\lambda_1=\epsilon/(1+\epsilon)$, a monotone
map of the ellipticity onto $[0,1)$, change by about 1\% or less and preserve
the ranking of structures, whereas a density-weighted mean is no better than
the plain one.

pydemi keeps every descriptor of the specification in its default output, so
that the columns of a descriptor table do not depend on these findings, but it
records them in the registry. The four descriptors whose median change exceeds
10\% for a reason other than a vanishing reference value, the two ellipticity
statistics and the two saddle counts, are tagged as fragile; the model-ready
feature set, which leaves them out, is one function call. An extension that is
off by default adds the bounded ellipticity as a converged alternative. Because
the sensitivity of each descriptor also differs between structures, we
recommend running the convergence report on representative structures of any
new dataset before relying on the Laplacian-, ELF- or topology-based
descriptors.

\section{Application to a 6,059-structure DFT dataset}
\label{sec:application}

The dataset comprises 6,059 VASP charge densities of 87 elements: 86
elemental solids, 3,195 intermetallics and 2,778 compounds with
electronegative elements (354 borides and carbides, 80 hydrides, 653
pnictides, 548 chalcogenides, 607 oxides and 536 halides). The 4,901 runs that
include their OUTCAR share one protocol (Supporting Information,
Table~S5): single-point PBE calculations with one PAW dataset per element, a
500\,eV plane-wave cutoff, collinear spin polarization, the tetrahedron
method with Bl\"ochl corrections and an automatic $k$-mesh with a spacing of
at most about 0.1\,\AA$^{-1}$; 4.5\% of them stopped at the maximum number of
electronic steps before reaching the $10^{-6}$\,eV criterion. The remaining
1,158 runs provide only the CHGCAR. No AECCAR or LOCPOT files are included and
an ELFCAR only for 86 runs, so every descriptor below is computed from the PAW
pseudo-density, with the electron localization function reconstructed from it
for all structures alike and the electronic Hartree potential obtained by
Fourier transform.
