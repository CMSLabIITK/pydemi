% --- ABSTRACT -------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
The electronic charge density $\rho(\mathbf{r})$ computed by density-functional
theory (DFT) encodes the bonding that materials-property models try to learn,
yet it is rarely used directly as a model input: machine-learning (ML)
featurizers are mostly built on composition and structure, and density-based
analyses such as the quantum theory of atoms in molecules are applied one
structure at a time. We present pydemi, an open-source Python framework that
turns volumetric charge densities into physically interpretable descriptors in
a single batch pipeline. pydemi is organized around one grid engine: a periodic
geometry pass and a small set of cached fields --- the density, the
magnetization, an electron localization function reconstructed from the
density, the electrostatic potential and the deformation density --- processed
by shared moment, shell-fraction, anisotropy, partition and topology
operations. The current release computes 222 scalar descriptors in 17
families, ranging from radial moments and Laplacian statistics to
site-resolved variance decompositions, spin-density measures, percolation
thresholds and a persistence-filtered census of critical points, each
documented in a machine-readable registry. pydemi reads densities from VASP
and, through cube and XSF files, from other plane-wave codes, and is designed
with a second input path for charge densities predicted by ML directly from
crystal structures.
% TODO: revise the previous sentence once the CIF -> CHGCAR model is integrated.
On a 6,059-structure VASP dataset we quantify how numerical choices affect
the descriptors --- voxel-count Laplacian descriptors differ between
discretizations by up to 32\% for individual structures, their
charge-weighted counterparts by at most 10\% --- and show that
projector-augmented-wave pseudo-densities require dedicated definitions: 61\%
of the densities are negative somewhere, pseudized atoms can lack a density
maximum at the nucleus, and 87\% of the deformation density lies inside the
augmentation spheres. The implementation is validated by 164 automated tests against
analytic densities, exact identities and reference data, and processes the
full dataset without failure.
\end{abstract}

\section{Numerical considerations}
\label{sec:numerics}

Every descriptor in pydemi is a reduction over a sampled density, so its value
depends on how derivatives are discretized, how voxels are assigned to atoms,
and how the underlying DFT code represents the density near the nuclei. This
section quantifies these effects on real VASP charge densities and describes
the definitions pydemi adopts as a result. Statistics are computed on random
samples of the 6,059-structure dataset described in
Section~\ref{sec:application} (grid spacings 0.060--0.070\,\AA); the analysis
scripts are distributed with the paper so that every number below can be
regenerated.

% --------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Derivatives on a periodic grid}
\label{sec:numerics:derivatives}

Gradients, Hessians and Laplacians are evaluated on the periodic grid with the
exact contravariant metric tensor, so that non-orthogonal cells are treated
without the cross-axis terms a diagonal approximation drops. Two
discretizations are provided: compact second-order central differences
(\texttt{fd}, the default) and exact derivatives of the trigonometric
interpolant (\texttt{spectral}). Two shortcuts that are easy to introduce in
practice change the result: computing the Laplacian by applying a
first-derivative routine twice, which produces a stencil of double width, and
using one-sided differences on the boundary planes of the cell, as
\texttt{numpy.gradient} does, which is incorrect for a periodic density.

Table~\ref{tab:derivatives} compares these discretizations on 200 random
structures, taking the spectral result as the reference. For the median
structure all methods agree to within 2\%, but the voxel-count fraction of
charge-concentration voxels, $f_{\nabla^2\rho<0} = N^{-1}\sum_k
\mathbb{1}(\nabla^2\rho_k < 0)$, differs by up to 21\% with the
double-width, one-sided stencil and by up to 32\% even between the compact
and spectral schemes. The origin is the voxel count itself: in flat
interstitial regions of metals many voxels have $\nabla^2\rho \approx 0$, and
their sign flips with small discretization differences. In YNi$_3$, for
example, $f_{\nabla^2\rho<0}$ is 0.111 with finite differences and 0.084
spectrally, whereas the charge-weighted fraction $f^{\rho}_{\nabla^2\rho<0} =
\sum_{\nabla^2\rho_k<0}\rho_k / \sum_k \rho_k$ is 0.511 and 0.502. Weighting by
the density suppresses the contribution of these marginal voxels: the charge-weighted
fraction is less sensitive in every statistic of Table~\ref{tab:derivatives},
by a factor of about two for the median structure and of 17 in the worst case
of the compact scheme. pydemi therefore reports it
alongside the voxel-count form.

The spectral Laplacian has a further limitation: its round-off error is
absolute, about $10^{-16}$ of the largest Laplacian in the cell, so in
near-empty regions its sign carries no information. For an isolated Gaussian
density in a 7\,\AA{} box, where the exact voxel fraction is 0.0079, finite
differences give 0.0079 but the spectral scheme gives 0.072, while the
charge-weighted fraction is correct with both (0.609 and 0.601 against 0.608).
Bulk crystals such as those of the present dataset contain little empty
space, but slabs, molecular crystals and porous frameworks do; for them the
finite-difference scheme or the charge-weighted fraction should be used.

One descriptor is affected not by discretization but by periodicity itself.
The Laplacian-concentration ratio $\sum_{\nabla^2\rho<0}|\nabla^2\rho_k| /
\sum_k|\nabla^2\rho_k|$ is identically $\tfrac{1}{2}$ for any periodic density:
by the divergence theorem the cell integral of $\nabla^2\rho$ vanishes, so its
negative and positive parts balance exactly. The same holds for the discrete
operators (the periodic stencil telescopes, and the spectral Laplacian has no
$\mathbf{G}=0$ component); across the 200 structures the integrated discrete
Laplacian never exceeds $3\times10^{-14}$. pydemi keeps this entry for
completeness and adds a variant restricted to the voxels outside the core
shell, where the flux through the shell boundary breaks the balance.

Finally, distances to the nearest nucleus are computed with a periodic
$k$-d tree whose image range is extended until it provably contains every
voxel's nearest atom. Wrapping fractional coordinate differences to the
nearest integer, a common shortcut, is the minimum image only for
near-orthogonal cells and overestimates distances by more than 0.1\,\AA{} in
strongly sheared ones.

\subsection{Partition schemes}
\label{sec:numerics:partitions}

Site-resolved descriptors require an assignment of voxels to atoms. pydemi
implements nearest-atom (Voronoi) and radius-weighted power-diagram
partitions, which are exact, and two smooth partitions whose weights must be
truncated in a periodic solid: Becke's fuzzy cells~\cite{becke1988} and
Hirshfeld's stockholder weights~\cite{hirshfeld1977}.

Becke's weight of atom $i$ is a product over all other atoms,
$P_i=\prod_{j\neq i}s(\mu_{ij})$, of a switching function that approaches 1
only as $1-O\big((d/R)^8\big)$ for a distant atom at distance $R$. In a
molecule the product is finite; in a periodic solid the number of atoms at
distance $R$ grows as $R^2$, and the weights converge only algebraically with
the number of atoms retained. Hirshfeld weights,
$w_i=\rho^{\mathrm{free}}_i/\sum_j\rho^{\mathrm{free}}_j$, inherit the
exponential decay of the free-atom densities instead.
Table~\ref{tab:partitions} shows the consequence for FeNi$_3$: the Becke
weights still carry an error of $10^{-3}$ with 300 neighbouring atoms in each
product, whereas the Hirshfeld weights are converged to $3\times10^{-6}$ with a
6.5\,\AA{} cutoff. We also found that the Becke products must be truncated
separately from the set of atoms that receive weight: the weight is always
carried by the eight nearest atom images (the remainder is below
$4\times10^{-10}$), but truncating the products at those eight atoms
overestimates the weights of the farthest ones, whose equidistant competitors
are then missing. Because converged Becke weights are expensive, pydemi makes
Becke partitioning opt-in and uses Hirshfeld as its default smooth partition.

The choice of partition also changes the descriptors themselves, because a
voxel's distance is measured to the atom it is assigned to.
Table~\ref{tab:partition-sensitivity} compares the whole-cell radial moment

and $f_{\mathrm{bond}}$ by a few per cent for the median structure; the

which should be kept in mind when it is interpreted physically. pydemi reports
the power-diagram and Hirshfeld variants of every partition-dependent
descriptor, so that this sensitivity can be assessed for any model.

\subsection{Projector-augmented-wave pseudo-densities}
\label{sec:numerics:paw}

The \texttt{CHGCAR} file written by VASP holds the pseudo-valence density plus
the compensation charge of the projector-augmented-wave (PAW)
method~\cite{blochl1994,kresse1999}, not the all-electron density. Inside each
augmentation sphere this density is pseudized, and three descriptor
definitions have to take that into account.

\paragraph{Negative densities.} In 3,680 of the 6,059 densities (61\%) the
pseudo-density is negative somewhere, and the median ratio of the density
minimum to the mean density is $-0.22$; in all but three of these structures
the negative values lie within $\max(c_2, R_{\mathrm{PAW}})$ of a nucleus
(defined below). The density floor
$\rho_{\min}/\langle\rho\rangle$, proposed as a metallicity criterion, is
therefore set by a PAW artefact rather than by the interstitial region.
pydemi adds an interstitial floor, the minimum over voxels farther than
$\max(c_2, R_{\mathrm{PAW}})$ from their nucleus, where $c_2$ is the
interstitial shell radius and $R_{\mathrm{PAW}}$ the outermost PAW cutoff
radius (\texttt{RCORE}) read from the \texttt{POTCAR} or \texttt{OUTCAR}. This
ratio is negative for only 3 structures, has a median of 0.13, and is
undefined for 8 densely packed cells in which no voxel lies outside the
spheres.

\paragraph{Pseudized nuclei.} A pseudized atom need not have a density maximum
at its nucleus. In CaSi$_3$Pt the density has maxima at the Ca and Pt nuclei
but not at the Si nuclei, where it is negative ($-0.18$\,e\,\AA$^{-3}$ at
0.02\,\AA{} from Si); the Si valence density instead forms eight lobes 0.81 and
0.83\,\AA{} from the Si nuclei, holding 6.3 electrons. With a fixed 0.8\,\AA{}
cutoff these lobes are counted as eight non-nuclear maxima. pydemi classifies a
maximum as non-nuclear only if it lies farther than $\max(c_1,
R_{\mathrm{PAW}})$ from its nearest nucleus (for Si, $R_{\mathrm{PAW}} =
1.00$\,\AA), since pseudization cannot move density maxima outside the
augmentation sphere; for CaSi$_3$Pt this leaves no non-nuclear maxima.

\paragraph{Deformation density.} The deformation density
$\Delta\rho=\rho-\sum_i\rho^{\mathrm{free}}_i$ compares the crystal density
with a superposition of free atoms. For a \texttt{CHGCAR} the free-atom
reference must contain only the valence electrons of the PAW dataset (ZVAL),
but it retains the all-electron shape inside the augmentation sphere.
Table~\ref{tab:feni3} shows the consequence around the Fe site of FeNi$_3$:
beyond 0.8\,\AA{} the two densities agree to within about 3\%, whereas within
0.8\,\AA{} of the nucleus, well inside the 1.22\,\AA{} Fe augmentation sphere,
the pseudization shifts about 0.45 electrons from $r<0.4$\,\AA{} to
0.4--0.8\,\AA. Over 121 random structures,
87\% of $\int|\Delta\rho|\,dV$ (median) lies inside the augmentation spheres,
which occupy 45\% of the volume, while the charge inside the spheres is
conserved to within 0.6\% (median; interquartile range $-1.4\%$ to $+0.6\%$).
Deformation descriptors weighted by $|\Delta\rho|$ over the whole cell are
therefore dominated by the pseudization, although $\Delta\rho$ outside the
spheres reflects the bonding. pydemi therefore computes the whole-cell
deformation-density family from the all-electron density (\texttt{AECCAR0} +
\texttt{AECCAR2}) against all-electron free atoms, which is consistent by
construction. When only a \texttt{CHGCAR} is available, it computes the same
quantities from the valence reference over the voxels outside the augmentation
spheres, where the pseudo-density equals the all-electron valence density
(augmentation radii from the \texttt{POTCAR} or \texttt{OUTCAR} for 4,901
structures, covalent radii otherwise). For the 6,059 structures these voxels
make up 53\% of the cell (median; 5th--95th percentile 21--76\%), and
the resulting deformation polarity $\int|\Delta\rho|\,dV/Q$ over these voxels is
2.3\% of the valence charge (median; 0.8--5.9\%).

The same mismatch affects atomic charges. Hirshfeld weights built from the
valence free-atom densities integrate a pure superposition of free atoms to
zero charges, as they must, but for the \texttt{CHGCAR} densities of 31 binary
compounds the sign of the resulting charge transfer follows the
electronegativity difference in only 58\% of cases (17 of the 26 binaries with
$\Delta\chi>0.4$). pydemi therefore reports Hirshfeld charges from a
\texttt{CHGCAR} with a warning, and recommends the all-electron density or a
reference built from isolated-atom calculations with the same PAW datasets, in
which the pseudization cancels.

\subsection{Ripple and topological persistence}
\label{sec:numerics:persistence}

Critical points are counted with a piecewise-linear Morse census on the
Freudenthal triangulation of the grid~\cite{freudenthal1942}, with ties
broken by voxel index. For this consistent census the alternating sum
$n_{\max}-n_{\mathrm{saddle},2}+n_{\mathrm{saddle},1}-n_{\min}$ equals the
Euler characteristic of the three-torus, zero, for any sampled
function~\cite{banchoff1970}; it is an implementation check rather than a
measure of grid adequacy, and it vanishes for every structure in the dataset.

The counts themselves are sensitive to low-amplitude ripple. In
nearly-free-electron metals the pseudo-valence density is almost flat, and
its small undulations produce many maxima away from the nuclei: 474
structures have at least one such maximum, Mg$_3$(TiAl$_9$)$_2$ has 1,028, and
Yb$_2$MgIn has 2,788.
Requiring the steepest-ascent basin of a maximum to hold at least 0.01
electrons is not sufficient, because a flat electron sea splits into many
basins that each hold more than that (576 in Mg$_3$(TiAl$_9$)$_2$). pydemi
therefore filters maxima by their topological
persistence~\cite{edelsbrunner2002}: a maximum is kept if the density must
fall by at least 10\% of its height before its superlevel-set component merges
with that of a higher maximum. The persistence is computed exactly from the
ascent basins, because two components merge at the highest point of the
boundary between their basins. A maximum that passes this filter is counted
only if its basin, together with the basins of the maxima absorbed into it,
holds at least 0.01 electrons. With these two criteria the number of structures
with non-nuclear maxima falls from 474 to 90, and ripple-dominated cases vanish
completely (YMg$_3$: 124 basin-filtered maxima, none persistent), while
well-separated interstitial maxima are retained, for example 32 maxima holding
90 of the 136 electrons in Mg$_3$(TiAl$_9$)$_2$. The charge criterion alone
changes only four structures. Low-charge persistent maxima remain in two Yb
compounds (18 maxima holding 0.63 electrons in YbPrZn$_2$, 18 holding 0.80 in
Yb$_2$GaHg); in YbPrZn$_2$ they lie 1.46\,\AA{} from the Zn nuclei, outside the
1.22\,\AA{} Zn augmentation sphere, and are therefore features of the density
rather than artefacts of the pseudization, and pydemi keeps them.

\subsection{Grid convergence}
\label{sec:numerics:convergence}

Whether a grid resolves a descriptor can be tested directly: pydemi recomputes
the descriptors on Fourier-coarsened copies of the grid and reports the
relative change of each. For 30 random structures, 20\% of the 102 quantities
in the report (median; range 12--39\%) change by more than 2\% when the number
of grid points along each axis is reduced to 80\%. The most sensitive are the
critical-point counts (median change 17--20\%), the ellipticity statistics
(29\% for the mean), the fractional charge anisotropy (13\%) and the ELF-based
anisotropy (9\%). The radial moments, the percolation thresholds, the local
energy densities, the information-theoretic measures, the Hartree-potential
and bond-midpoint descriptors, the anisotropy-tensor eigenvalues and the
interstitial density floor change by about 0.1\% or less.

The within-element site variances show the largest relative changes, but for a
different reason: when all sites of an element are equivalent by symmetry,
their true variance is zero and the computed value is numerical noise. This
is the case for 3,716 of the 5,633 structures with repeated elements, where
the within-element variance of $m_1$ has a median of $4\times10^{-8}$\,\AA$^2$,
about 300 times below its median for structures with inequivalent sites.
These descriptors carry information only for chemically or structurally
disordered cells, and their relative grid sensitivity should be read against
this noise level. Because the sensitivity of each descriptor differs between
structures, we recommend running the convergence report on representative
structures of any dataset before relying on the Laplacian-, ELF- or
topology-based descriptors.
